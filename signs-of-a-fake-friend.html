10 Signs of a Fake Friend

Last updated: October 13, 2026 • By Fake Friend Tester Relationship Care
Important Note: While our fake friend test quiz is a lighthearted social game made strictly for entertainment, true emotional well-being requires understanding real interpersonal patterns. Look for repeated behavior over weeks or months, not a single off day.

Friendships should bring mutual joy, emotional safety, and shared encouragement into your life. When a friendship consistently leaves you feeling drained, insecure, or taken advantage of, it may be time to evaluate whether that person is genuinely invested in your bond. Here are 10 common warning signs of a fake friend and compassionate ways to respond without drama.

1. They Only Reach Out When They Need a Favor

A fake friend treats communication as a transaction. When they need notes for a class, a ride to the airport, or access to your contacts, they flood your phone with affectionate messages. Once their need is met, they go radio silent.

Example:

Texting "Hey bestie! So sorry I've been MIA, but can you lend me $40 until Friday?" after ignoring your messages for three weeks.

💡 How to respond: Politely decline favors with clear, neutral boundaries ("I won't be able to help with that this time") and notice if they maintain contact when favors are off the table.
2. They Disappear When You Are Going Through Hardship

True friends show up during rain, not just sunshine. A fake friend loves being around for parties, celebrations, and fun weekends, but suddenly has "too much on their plate" whenever you are sick, grieving, or dealing with personal setbacks.

Example:

Leaving you on delivered when you text that you are having an anxiety attack, but posting Instagram stories hanging out at a café two hours later.

💡 How to respond: Lean into friends and family who consistently offer a listening ear. Stop expending emotional energy trying to seek comfort from someone who isn't available.
3. They Gossip About Others in Front of You

Psychological research confirms that if someone routinely tears down their other friends, mocks their secrets, or shares their private DMs with you, they are almost certainly doing the exact same thing to you when you leave the room.

Example:

Saying "Can you believe what Sarah wore today? She looked ridiculous," immediately after hugging Sarah goodbye.

💡 How to respond: Refuse to participate in gossip. Change the topic or respond with a gentle neutral remark ("I actually like that jacket"). Never share deep secrets with habitual gossips.
4. They Downplay or Minimize Your Achievements

Authentic friends celebrate your victories as if they were their own. A fake friend harbors subtle envy and will either ignore your good news, change the subject to themselves, or offer backhanded compliments designed to take you down a peg.

Example:

When you share that you got a promotion, they reply: "Congrats! Honestly they give those out to anyone after a year anyway."

💡 How to respond: Celebrate your wins with people who are genuinely happy for you. A backhanded compliment speaks entirely about their insecurity, not your merit.
5. Conversations Are Exclusively One-Sided

In a healthy relationship, there is a natural conversational rhythm of give and take. With an insincere friend, 95% of every conversation revolves around their drama, their feelings, and their problems. The moment you begin sharing your day, they interrupt or lose interest.

Example:

Listening to them vent for 45 minutes, only for them to look at their watch and say "Anyway, I gotta run!" the second you start answering how you are doing.

💡 How to respond: Notice if they ever ask thoughtful follow-up questions about your life. If not, preserve your energy and shorten your check-ins.
6. They Disguise Cruel Insults as "Just a Joke"

Humor and friendly banter are natural parts of friendship, but there is a clear line between affectionate teasing and hurtful jabs. A fake friend will target your deepest insecurities in public and dismiss your hurt feelings with "You're way too sensitive."

💡 How to respond: Calmly ask them to explain the joke: "What makes that funny?" When cruelty is stripped of laughter, the disrespect becomes obvious.
7. They Frequently Flake or Cancel Plans Last Minute

Life happens and schedules change, but consistent flaking signals that you are viewed as a backup plan. If they often cancel plans an hour before meeting because "something came up," they may have taken a more attractive social offer.

💡 How to respond: Stop being the sole initiator. Let them propose and confirm the next hangout. If they don't, the silence tells you everything you need to know.
8. They Compete With You Rather Than Collaborate

Everything becomes an unspoken contest. Whether it is grades, dating, clothes, or followers, they need to feel one step ahead. A real friend walks beside you; an envious friend constantly checks to make sure they are in front.

💡 How to respond: Opt out of the competition entirely. You don't have to defend your choices or out-do anyone.
9. They Disrespect Your Boundaries and Pressure You

When you say "no" to an invitation, a loan, or a favor, an authentic friend respects your choice. An insincere friend reacts with guilt trips, passive-aggressive silent treatment, or accusations that you are not being a good friend.

💡 How to respond: Stand firm in your decisions. A boundary is not a negotiation; anyone who gets angry at your healthy boundary was benefiting from your lack of one.
10. You Feel Mentally and Emotionally Exhausted Around Them

Your intuition is a powerful diagnostic tool. If you regularly feel a pit in your stomach before seeing someone, or leave hangouts feeling drained and second-guessing yourself, your body is picking up on an unhealthy dynamic.

💡 How to respond: Trust your feelings. You don't need a formal confrontation or an explosive argument—you can quietly step back, reduce communication, and prioritize friends who bring peace.
